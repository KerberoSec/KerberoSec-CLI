### 🧠 What Is SOC Alert Triage?

SOC alert triage is the process of reviewing security alerts and determining what happened, why the alert was generated, whether the activity is suspicious, and what action should be taken.

A SOC environment can receive hundreds or thousands of alerts.

Not every alert represents a real security incident.

The analyst therefore needs to prioritize alerts and investigate the available evidence before making a decision.

```Simplified SOC Alert-Triage Process
Security Events
      ↓
Detection Rules
      ↓
Security Alerts
      ↓
SOC Alert Queue
      ↓
Analyst Investigation
      ↓
Classification
      ↓
┌───────────────────┐
│                   │
↓                   ↓
True Positive    False Positive
↓                   ↓
Escalate          Document
↓                   ↓
Case/Incident     Close
└─────────┬─────────┘
          ↓
      Resolution
```

The goal is to identify meaningful security activity while reducing unnecessary investigation and alert fatigue.

### 🔎True Positive vs False Positive

Understanding the difference between True Positive and False Positive alerts is an important SOC skill.

True Positive

A True Positive (TP) occurs when a security detection correctly identifies suspicious or malicious activity.

``` Example:

Suspicious Activity
       ↓
Security Detection
       ↓
Investigation
       ↓
Evidence confirms malicious activity
       ↓
TRUE POSITIVE
False Positive
```

A False Positive (FP) occurs when a security detection triggers, but the investigation determines that the activity is legitimate or does not represent a security threat.

```Example:

Security Detection
       ↓
Investigation
       ↓
Legitimate Activity
       ↓
FALSE POSITIVE
```

Too many false positives can create alert fatigue, where analysts spend too much time investigating harmless activity.

For this reason, accurate alert classification and proper investigation are important parts of SOC operations.

**4. Analyzing Potential Phishing Indicators**

After opening the alert, I reviewed the email content rather than relying only on the alert title.

Several characteristics stood out.

🚩 Unexpected Inheritance Claim

The email contained an unexpected inheritance story.

Unexpected financial or inheritance claims can be used as social-engineering techniques to convince a recipient to respond.

🚩 Request for Banking Information

The message requested sensitive financial information.

Requests for banking or other sensitive information should be carefully validated before any information is provided.

🚩 External Sender

The email originated from an external domain.

An external sender does not automatically mean malicious activity, but it becomes important context when combined with other indicators.

🚩 Unusual Subject

The subject used an unrealistic inheritance scenario designed to attract the recipient's attention.

These indicators made the alert worth investigating further.

🔍 Phishing Investigation Mindset

```During an email investigation, I found it useful to ask:

Who sent the email?
        ↓
Who received it?
        ↓
What is the subject?
        ↓
What does the email request?
        ↓
Are there links or attachments?
        ↓
Does the sender/domain appear trustworthy?
        ↓
Are there other related alerts?
        ↓
Does additional evidence support the detection?
        ↓
True Positive or False Positive?
```

The important lesson is that the alert itself is only the starting point.

The analyst needs to examine the underlying evidence before making a final decision.

A weak investigation note would simply say:

"This is a false positive."

A stronger investigation note explains the evidence and reasoning behind the classification.

For example:

"The alert was reviewed against the available email evidence. The sender, message content, recipient, and requested information were analyzed to determine whether the activity represented legitimate communication or suspicious phishing behavior."

The goal is to make the investigation understandable to another analyst who may review the case later.

**11. Understanding Parent-Child Process Relationships**

A parent-child process relationship shows which process started another process.

```For example:

powershell.exe
      |
      +---- net.exe
      |
      +---- whoami.exe
```

This type of telemetry is valuable during endpoint investigations.

Attackers may use legitimate Windows utilities after gaining access to a system.

```For example:

whoami

can provide information about the current user.

Similarly:

net
```

can be used to query Windows accounts, groups, and other system information.

However:

The presence of these commands does not automatically prove malicious activity.

They can also be used legitimately by administrators, troubleshooting tools, and security software.

Therefore, the analyst should investigate the surrounding context before making a final determination.

**12. Questions to Ask During Process Investigation**

When investigating a suspicious process relationship, useful questions include:

Which user launched PowerShell?
        ↓
What command was executed?
        ↓
Was the activity expected?
        ↓
What process started it?
        ↓
What happened before the process?
        ↓
What happened after the process?
        ↓
Are there authentication events?
        ↓
Are there network events?
        ↓
Are there other related alerts?

This is an important part of endpoint security monitoring.

A single process may look harmless when viewed by itself.

The process chain and surrounding events can provide much more context.
