# Intro to Cyber Threat Intelligence

> **Platform:** TryHackMe  
> **Difficulty:** Easy  
> **Category:** Threat Intelligence / SOC / Blue Team

## Overview

This repository documents my learning experience from the **Intro to Cyber Threat Intelligence** room on TryHackMe. The room introduces the fundamentals of Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI), including how threat intelligence is collected, analysed, and used by Security Operations Centers (SOCs) to detect, investigate, and respond to cyber threats.

Rather than focusing on individual tools, this room builds a foundation for understanding the intelligence lifecycle and how actionable threat intelligence supports security operations.

---

## Objectives

- Understand the purpose of Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI).
- Learn the different types of threat intelligence.
- Explore the intelligence lifecycle.
- Understand Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) and Indicators of Attack (IOAs).
- Learn how threat intelligence supports SOC operations.
- Identify common threat intelligence sources and frameworks.

---

## Learning Methodology

During this room, I approached each task by understanding **why** threat intelligence is valuable rather than memorizing definitions.

My learning process included:

- Understanding how threat intelligence differs from raw security data.
- Learning how intelligence is collected, processed, and shared.
- Exploring the different intelligence levels used by organizations.
- Studying how IOCs and IOAs assist during investigations.
- Understanding the role of CTI throughout the incident response lifecycle.
- Reviewing common intelligence frameworks and industry standards.

This approach helped me understand how threat intelligence enables proactive defense instead of simply reacting to security incidents.

## Key Takeaways

One of the biggest lessons from this room was understanding that **data alone is not intelligence**. Threat intelligence is created by collecting, analysing, and contextualising information so that defenders can make informed security decisions.

I also learned that effective SOC investigations rely not only on log analysis but also on integrating external and internal intelligence sources to identify attacker infrastructure, techniques, and emerging threats.

The **Intro to Cyber Threat Intelligence** room provided a strong foundation in the principles of Cyber Threat Intelligence and its role in modern security operations.
